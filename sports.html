The card's record

Fresh scoreboard -- the pick-first card started Aug 19, 2026 and grades itself from day one. Every shown pick lands here as a WIN or LOSS at $1 a slip, settled by Kalshi itself. Give it a few weeks before trusting it with real beer money.
How this card works, in one breath: the sharpest sportsbooks in the world publish their opinion as prices; we strip out their commission to get an honest probability, then check what Kalshi's crowd charges for the same thing. When the crowd sells the sharps' pick at least 4¢ under the sharps' own number (after Kalshi's fee), it makes the card. We never pick against the sharps, never chase a "cheap" longshot, and anything over +15¢ is treated as bad data and suppressed. Props (first-5-innings, totals, strikeouts) are the main event -- moneylines tag along. The card covers MLB, NFL, college football, the NBA (once its games are inside the scan window) and tour-level tennis; golf is deliberately absent because the odds feed carries only tournament-winner longshots for it -- no matchup lines means no honest pick, so none is invented. Sharps must lean at least 55% -- coin flips don't get picks. Every market we evaluate is logged to sports_picks.csv, shown or not, and every shown pick is graded by Kalshi's own settlement in sports_results.csv. The record above is the only reason to trust (or ignore) this card.

The parlay board answers a different question: not "what's mispriced" but "who are today's most likely winners". Legs are the sharps' strongest full-game or full-match favorites, from any league on the card (70%+ after removing the books' commission), Kalshi's price plays no part in choosing them, and each leg must match a hand-verified Kalshi market so the board can be graded by Kalshi's own settlement — hit or miss, in parlay_results.csv. The stated combined chance is the honest multiplied probability; there's no dollar score on purpose, because every sportsbook pays parlays differently. This board, like everything here, never bets. You do (or don't). The team stacks are the one section that bundles legs from a SINGLE game on purpose — one slip per team (each player's team read off Kalshi's own ticker, never guessed), plus the whole-game slip when it adds legs — built for a sportsbook's same-game parlay, since Kalshi has no parlay ticket. Their stated % is the plain independent product, printed as a reference with the direction of its error said out loud, and their graded record (the TEAM/GAME ids in parlay_results.csv) measures what the same-game correlation is actually worth.

The combo board is the parlay board with every sector of this operation invited: the sharps' strongest game favorites plus the weather bot's own strongest bracket picks, stacked tallest-payout first. A weather leg has to pass TWO experts at once — the ensemble must put 70%+ of its members on the bracket AND Kalshi's live market must bid 70¢+ for it — because the record shows the ensemble alone runs overconfident; the leg then states the LOWER of the two numbers, so the board understates on purpose. Cities benched by the scoreboard never supply a leg. One honest wrinkle: weather picks in the same air mass can win or lose together, so a stack heavy on nearby cities is riskier than the multiplied number implies — the combo record above is the judge of that, stated chance vs hit rate. And the plain truth about "high paying": the payout and the chance are the same number upside down — the only honest way to a bigger payout is stacking MORE real favorites, never reaching for longer shots.
