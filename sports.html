When an offense gets rolling, the quarterback's yards and his catchers' yards come from the same drives — these legs tend to hit TOGETHER, which is exactly why your book sells this as a same-game parlay. Which team each player is on comes straight off Kalshi's own market ticker, never a guess. The WHOLE GAME slip appears when it adds legs beyond one team — in a high-scoring game both offenses' numbers rise together. The stated chance still multiplies the legs like separate coin flips, because that is the only number we can state without inventing one: for legs riding the same offense the real chance is usually HIGHER than stated, while two receivers fighting for the same targets pull it a little lower — the record below measures which way it really leans. The card's record

Fresh scoreboard -- the pick-first card started Aug 19, 2026 and grades itself from day one. Every shown pick lands here as a WIN or LOSS at $1 a slip, settled by Kalshi itself. Give it a few weeks before trusting it with real beer money.
How this card works, in one breath: the sharpest sportsbooks in the world publish their opinion as prices; we strip out their commission to get an honest probability, then check what Kalshi's crowd charges for the same thing. When the crowd sells the sharps' pick at least 4¢ under the sharps' own number (after Kalshi's fee), it makes the card. We never pick against the sharps, never chase a "cheap" longshot, and anything over +15¢ is treated as bad data and suppressed. Props (first-5-innings, totals, strikeouts) are the main event -- moneylines tag along. The card covers MLB, NFL, college football, the NBA (once its games are inside the scan window) and tour-level tennis; golf is deliberately absent because the odds feed carries only tournament-winner longshots for it -- no matchup lines means no honest pick, so none is invented. Sharps must lean at least 55% -- coin flips don't get picks. Every market we evaluate is logged to sports_picks.csv, shown or not, and every shown pick is graded by Kalshi's own settlement in sports_results.csv. The record above is the only reason to trust (or ignore) this card.

The parlay board answers a different question: not "what's mispriced" but "who are today's most likely winners". Legs are the sharps' strongest full-game or full-match favorites, from any league on the card (70%+ after removing the books' commission), Kalshi's price plays no part in choosing them, and each leg must match a hand-verified Kalshi market so the board can be graded by Kalshi's own settlement — hit or miss, in parlay_results.csv. The stated combined chance is the honest multiplied probability; there's no dollar score on purpose, because every sportsbook pays parlays differently. This board, like everything here, never bets. You do (or don't).
